A vector embedding represents a piece of text as a list of numbers, placed
so that texts with similar meaning end up close together in that numeric
space. A sentence embedding model produces one such vector per sentence or
passage, trained so that semantically related passages have a small
distance between their vectors even when they share few or no exact words.
Cosine similarity, which measures the angle between two vectors rather than
their raw distance, is the usual way to score how related two embeddings
are, since it is unaffected by vector length. Embedding an entire document
corpus once, ahead of time, turns search into a geometry problem: a new
query is embedded the same way, and the documents whose vectors are
closest to it are returned as the most relevant matches.
