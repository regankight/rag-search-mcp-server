Retrieval-augmented generation combines a search step with a language model's
generation step. Instead of relying only on what a model learned during
training, the system first retrieves relevant passages from an external
corpus and then conditions the model's answer on that retrieved text. This
keeps answers grounded in real source material, makes it possible to update
the system's knowledge by changing the corpus rather than retraining the
model, and lets the response cite exactly which passages it drew from. The
retrieval step is usually built on vector embeddings and a similarity
search, though keyword search and hybrid combinations of both are common
too. The quality of the final answer is bounded by the quality of what gets
retrieved: a strong language model given irrelevant context will still
produce a weak or hallucinated answer.
