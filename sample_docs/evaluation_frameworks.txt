Evaluating a retrieval system means measuring whether the documents it
returns are actually the right ones, not just inspecting a few examples by
eye. Standard metrics include recall at k, the fraction of known relevant
documents that appear in the top k results, and mean reciprocal rank, which
rewards relevant documents appearing earlier in the ranking. Normalized
discounted cumulative gain extends this to graded relevance rather than a
simple relevant-or-not judgment. Building a labeled evaluation set, where
each query has known relevant documents attached, is usually the hardest
and most valuable part of the process, since the scoring itself is
mechanical once labels exist. Established open-source evaluation
frameworks package these metrics together with dataset handling and
reporting, which matters for comparability across projects and for
catching regressions when a retrieval pipeline changes.
