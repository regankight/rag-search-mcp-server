Hybrid search combines two different ways of finding relevant documents:
sparse keyword matching, such as BM25, and dense vector similarity from
embeddings. Keyword search is strong at exact terms, rare names, and
identifiers that an embedding model may represent poorly. Dense retrieval is
strong at paraphrases, synonyms, and conceptual matches where no exact
keyword overlaps. Because the two approaches make different kinds of
mistakes, combining their ranked results often retrieves documents that
either method alone would miss. Reciprocal rank fusion is a common way to
merge the two ranked lists: each document gets a score based on its rank
position in each list, and the combined score determines the final
ordering. This avoids needing to calibrate the two systems' raw scores
against each other, which are usually on very different numeric scales.
